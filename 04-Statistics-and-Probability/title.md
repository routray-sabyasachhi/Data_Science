# Statistics and Probability

## Why study this
Statistics is how we reason from incomplete and noisy observations. It helps distinguish signal from chance, quantify uncertainty, design studies, and avoid confident but unsupported conclusions.

## Topics and subtopics
- Data and measurement: populations/samples, observational units, variables, scales, sampling frames, measurement error, missingness
- Descriptive statistics: tables, proportions, mean/median/mode, quantiles, variance/standard deviation, robust summaries
- Visualization for distributions: histograms, ECDF, box/violin plots, scatterplots, stratification
- Probability: events, axioms, conditional probability, independence, Bayes theorem, counting
- Random variables: discrete/continuous, PMF/PDF/CDF, expectation, variance, covariance, transformations
- Distributions: Bernoulli, binomial, geometric, Poisson, uniform, normal, exponential, t, chi-square, F; when assumptions fit
- Sampling: random sampling, sampling distributions, standard error, CLT, bootstrap, permutation methods
- Estimation: point estimators, bias, variance, consistency, likelihood, maximum likelihood, method of moments
- Confidence intervals: interpretation, construction, bootstrap intervals, multiple comparisons
- Hypothesis tests: null/alternative, test statistic, p-values, significance, power, Type I/II errors, effect sizes, practical significance
- Tests: z/t tests, paired tests, proportion tests, chi-square, ANOVA; assumptions and diagnostics
- Correlation and association: Pearson, Spearman, confounding, Simpson's paradox, correlation limits
- Regression inference: simple/multiple linear regression, coefficient uncertainty, residuals, assumptions, multicollinearity
- Categorical outcomes: logistic regression, odds and probabilities, calibration
- Bayesian statistics: priors, likelihood, posterior, credible intervals, conjugacy intuition, Bayesian computation overview
- Study design: randomization, blocking, blinding, sampling bias, observational vs experimental data
- Missing data and outliers: MCAR/MAR/MNAR concepts, robust analysis, sensitivity
- Multiple testing: family-wise error, false discovery rate, preregistration
- Statistical communication: uncertainty intervals, assumptions, limitations, avoid causal claims from association
