# Time Series, Experiments, and Causal Methods

## Why study this
Temporal and intervention questions need methods that respect time, dependence, and confounding. This subject helps forecast responsibly and evaluate whether an action caused a change.

## Topics and subtopics
- Time series structure: timestamps, frequency, trend, seasonality, cycles, noise, stationarity
- Time data handling: time zones, calendars, resampling, missing periods, event time vs processing time
- Forecasting: naive baselines, moving averages, exponential smoothing, ARIMA concepts, regression features, ML forecasting
- Validation: chronological splits, rolling-origin evaluation, horizon-specific metrics, leakage prevention
- Forecast diagnostics: residuals, autocorrelation, prediction intervals, changing regimes
- Experiments: hypothesis, random assignment, control/treatment, randomization units, blocking, blinding
- A/B testing: metrics, sample size/power, duration, guardrails, sequential testing pitfalls, multiple testing
- Causal reasoning: potential outcomes, counterfactuals, DAGs, confounders, mediators, colliders, selection bias
- Observational methods: matching, regression adjustment, propensity scores, inverse weighting, assumptions
- Quasi-experiments: difference-in-differences, regression discontinuity, instrumental variables, interrupted time series
- Heterogeneous treatment effects and uplift concepts
- Sensitivity analysis, robustness, pre-registration, ethical constraints
- Communication: causal assumptions, uncertainty, external validity, practical effect sizes
